How to do it
1

Set the bar on the rack at upper chest height. Step under it so the bar rests on your upper traps — not your neck. Grip slightly wider than shoulder width.

2

Unrack and step back with two steps. Feet shoulder-width apart, toes pointed out 15–30 degrees. This is your stance — keep it consistent.

3

Brace before you move. Take a big breath into your belly, brace your core like you're about to take a punch, and squeeze your lats. This stiffens your torso.

4

Descend by pushing your hips back and bending your knees at the same time. Chest stays up. Knees track over your toes. Go until hips reach parallel or below.

5

Drive back up by pushing the floor away. Think "chest up, hips forward." Keep knees tracking over toes. Exhale as you pass the hard point.

6

Re-brace between reps. At the top, take a breath and brace again before the next rep. Don't rush — this is where most beginners lose tension.

Say these in your head during the set
"Chest up"
"Knees out"
"Push the floor away"
"Big breath, brace, then move"
Common mistakes
Knees caving inward

The most common beginner mistake. Your knees collapse toward each other on the way up, especially when it gets heavy. This strains the knee joint.

Fix: Actively push your knees out as you ascend. Think "spread the floor" with your feet.
Hips rising faster than chest

Hips shoot up on the ascent and the lift turns into a good morning. Usually caused by going too heavy too soon or weak quads.

Fix: Think "chest up" as you drive out of the bottom. Reduce weight until form holds.
Not bracing before the rep

Squatting without a proper brace leaves your spine unsupported under load. Fine at light weights — dangerous as you progress.

Fix: Make it a ritual. Breath → brace → move. Every single rep, every single set.
Can't do this? Try instead
Goblet Squat

Easier to learn the squat pattern. The weight in front acts as a counterbalance, making it natural to sit upright. Use a dumbbell or kettlebell.
